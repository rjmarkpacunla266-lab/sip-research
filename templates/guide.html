How to use Sturch

A short, friendly guide to every tool. Pick a topic below, or read from the top. Sturch is free, and nothing in it is written by AI: every answer is a real sentence from a real paper.

Start here

🚀 Quick start in 4 steps

You can be reading your first paper in about a minute.

  1. Get in. Sign up with just a username and password (no email needed), or try Sturch as a guest first. Guest searches and saved papers are kept for 30 days, and you can turn a guest into a full account any time.
  2. Search. Open Search Papers, type your topic, and press Enter.
  3. Read and cite. Tap a title to read it, or tap Cite to copy a reference in APA, MLA, Chicago or Harvard style.
  4. Save. Tap 🔖 Save on papers you want to use. Find them again in Saved.
Tip: Not sure where to begin? Use the Topic Finder to explore a subject and find a research question, then come back to Search Papers for the details.

Tool 1

🔍 Search Papers

Searches OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Crossref and Europe PMC at the same time, so you do not have to visit each one.

Open Search Papers

Simple mode (the default)

  1. Type what you are looking for in the box and press Enter or tap Search. No tags needed. Simple mode matches your words against paper titles only, so the results stay on topic.
  2. Narrow it down with the three filters under the box:
    • Date: Any time, or since a recent year.
    • Sort: Most cited (famous, well-tested papers) or Most recent (newest work).
    • Free to read only: shows only open-access papers you can read in full without paying.
  3. Look at each result card. You see the title, authors, journal, year, a short snippet, how many times it was cited, and a green Free to read mark when it is open access.
  4. Use the links under a card.
ButtonWhat it does

🔖 SaveKeeps the paper in your Saved list.

Only 10 results show at first. Tap Show next 10 to see more, and Load more papers to fetch a fresh batch.

Advanced mode

Switch on Advanced mode in the top corner when you need more control. Sturch remembers your choice, and your search stays when you switch.

  • Tags: type a keyword and press Enter to add it as a tag. Add several tags for a sharper search, and tap the × on a tag to remove it.
  • From year / To year: only papers published in that range.
  • Sort by: Most cited or Most recent.
  • Word in: look for your words in the title and abstract (the default here), the title only, or the abstract only. Use this when you also want papers that mention your topic only in the abstract.
  • Open access only: same as "Free to read only" in Simple mode.
  • Sources: turn databases on or off. For example, keep only PubMed for medicine topics, or only arXiv for computer science and physics.
  • Expand All / Collapse All / Copy All APA: open every card, close them, or copy every reference at once.
  • Add to collection (📚+), Share (🔗) and Export: organize, share, or download your results.
Heads up: going back to Simple mode resets the Advanced-only options (end year, sources, and the Word in setting). Simple mode always searches titles only. Sturch tells you when options are reset.

Search tips

  • Use specific words. biofuel flammability finds far better papers than energy.
  • Too few results? Remove a filter, or try a related word.
  • Too many results? Add a second word, or choose a recent date.
  • Need to understand the basics first? A paper with "review" in its title usually explains the whole topic.
Tool 2

📄 Reading a paper and citing it

Tap Read or a title in your results to open the paper reader.

  • Full text or abstract: Sturch loads the full paper when a free copy is available, and the abstract when it is not. A note above the text tells you which one you are reading.
  • Find in paper: type a word in the bar at the top (or press Ctrl + F). Matches are highlighted, and the arrows jump between them.
  • Citation styles: choose APA, MLA, Chicago or Harvard, then tap Copy.
  • Related papers: finds more papers on the same topic.
  • Save, Share, Print, or Download as note: keep the paper, send a link, or download a short text note with the reference and abstract.
  • Dark or light mode: tap the 🌙 button at the top.
Always check references. Citations are made automatically from the paper's data. Some may be missing a volume, issue or page number. A warning appears when Sturch knows something is missing. Compare with the original paper before you hand in your work.
Tool 3

💡 Answer Finder TIPS

Ask a question and get sentences from real papers that answer it, each linked to its source. It is not AI: it finds and shows sentences that already exist.

Open Answer Finder
  1. Pick a question type (the pills at the top): What is, What are, What causes, Effect of, How does, Why is, Define, Difference between, Benefits of, Risks of, Examples of, How to, or Is … safe?
  2. Type your topic. Or just type a whole question, like why do coral reefs matter.
    What causes → inflation
    Risks of → caffeine
    Difference between → mitosis and meiosis
  3. Press Ask. The answers appear with the words you asked about highlighted. A gold ★ Best answer tag marks the strongest match.
  4. Check "I read this as…". Sturch tells you how it understood your question. If that is wrong, tap one of the other readings.
  5. Use an answer:
    • Show context: reads the sentences before and after, so you do not use a sentence out of context.
    • Copy with citation: copies the sentence with an in-text citation and a reference.
    • Save paper: keeps the source paper in your library.

Not happy with the answers?

Tap Continue searching (1 min). Sturch reads for up to a minute more, going past the papers it has already read, and adds new answers below under "Round 2", "Round 3" and so on. You can repeat it as many times as you like, or tap Stop to end a round early.

Tip: If you only get one weak answer, try a different question type for the same topic, such as "Effect of" instead of "What causes".
Be careful: the Answer Finder reads abstracts and open-access text, so it can miss things or pick a sentence that is only partly right. Read the source before you rely on an answer.
Tool 4

🧾 Source Tracer TIPS

Found a sentence or quote and need to know which paper it came from? Paste it here.

Open Source Tracer
  1. Paste the quote exactly as you found it. A full sentence works better than a few words.
  2. Tap Quick Trace for a fast first answer. It checks open-access papers only, so it is quick but less thorough, and it is labelled that way.
  3. Or run the full trace for a deeper search. It can take around a minute and a half, so keep the page open.
  4. Read the result. You get the most likely paper, with a confidence level of high, medium or low, and a few other possible matches.
Tip: If the confidence is low, do not cite the paper yet. Open it, find your sentence in the text, and make sure it is really there.
Tool 5

🧠 Topic Finder TIPS

Helps you find a research topic that is not too big and not too small, and shows you what has and has not been studied.

Open Topic Finder
  1. Enter a subject. Be specific: biofuel works better than biology. If your subject is too broad, Sturch suggests more specific ones to tap.
  2. Choose your level (High School, College or Graduate). The filters are set for you.
  3. Add a focus and keywords if you have an angle in mind, such as flammability.
  4. Read the topic size meter. It shows how many papers contain all your words, from No match and Very narrow to Good size, Broad and Too broad. Aim for Good size.
  5. Narrow it step by step. Tap a sub-topic to add it to your keywords. The trail at the top records each step with its paper count, and you can tap any step to go back.
  6. Study the results:
    • Papers per year: shows when the topic grew. Tap a bar to see only that year.
    • Methods seen: how other researchers studied it, such as surveys, experiments or simulations. Tap one to see those papers.
    • Research gaps: sentences where authors say something is unknown, missing or needs more study. Filter them by type. These are good starting points for an original question.
    • 🌍 Check local research: pick a country from the list next to the button, then see how many papers mention that country or have authors there. Sturch remembers your country. A low number can point to a local gap.
  7. Keep your work: tap 🔖 Save on papers, and use Copy topic brief or Download .txt to take your sub-topics, gaps and references with you.
Tip: A good school topic is usually a Good size topic with at least one clear gap, that you can actually study with the time and tools you have.
Remember: these results are a map, not your topic. Your final research question should come from your own thinking, checked with your teacher.

Study smart

✅ Good habits for school work

  • Check every citation against the paper before you submit.
  • Read more than the abstract. Use the full text when it is free.
  • Use several papers, not just the first result. Newer and well-cited papers are usually good places to start.
  • Quote carefully. Use "Show context" in the Answer Finder so you do not change what the author meant.
  • Write in your own words and always credit the source.
  • Ask your teacher which citation style your school wants (APA is the most common).
Stuck?

🛟 Help and common questions

Is Sturch really free?

Yes. Searching, the tools, and saving papers are free for students. If you like it, you can support it from the Donate page.

Does Sturch use AI to write the answers?

No. The Answer Finder and Topic Finder find and show sentences that already exist in published papers, using fixed rules. Every answer links to the paper it came from.

I got no results. What can I do?

Try fewer or simpler words, remove the date or "Free to read only" filter, or switch to Advanced mode and make sure all sources are on. Simple mode only looks at titles, so if your words appear only in abstracts, use Advanced mode and set Word in to title and abstract.

Why can't I read the full paper?

Many papers are behind a paywall. Sturch only shows the full text when a free copy exists. You can turn on "Free to read only" to see just those papers, or ask your school library for access.

The search or a tool is slow.

Sturch searches several databases at once, so a search can take a few seconds. Tools that read full papers, like the full Source Tracer, can take around a minute and a half. Keep the page open and wait for it to finish.

How many papers can Sturch search?

See the live count on the home page. It is taken from each database and shows a range, because the same paper can appear in more than one database.

How does Sturch list authors in a reference?

Each style has its own rule. APA lists every author up to 20 (with & before the last one). MLA uses the first author and "et al." for three or more. Chicago lists up to 10 authors, then the first seven and "et al." Harvard lists up to three, then the first author and "et al." BibTeX keeps every author. Some schools use slightly different rules, so check what your teacher asks for.

I forgot my password.

Use the recovery code you were given when you signed up, on the login page's reset option. Keep that code somewhere safe.

I saw something wrong or a bug.

Sturch is free and rule-based, so mistakes can still happen. Tell your teacher or the person who shared Sturch with you, and check any important fact in the original paper.
