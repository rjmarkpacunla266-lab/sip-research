Before you use Sturch

Sturch 1.0 is out. It is still improving. Results can be incomplete, and a data source can be temporarily unavailable.

Answers and matches are not proof. The Answer Finder and Source Tracer read paper abstracts, which can leave out context. A sentence can be correct in its paper and still not fit your question. Open the paper before you quote or cite it.

Topic Finder gives ideas, not approved topics. Check with your teacher or adviser before you commit to a topic.

Don't spam the search button. Wait for results to load before searching again. Use Load More to get more papers on the same topic.

Always verify references before submitting. APA references in Sturch are auto-generated. They may have missing volume, issue, or page numbers. Double-check before academic submission.

For research assistance only. Sturch helps you find and cite papers. It does not write your research for you.

Data is from public academic databases. Sturch does not own any papers. All content belongs to their respective publishers and authors.

Not professional advice. Papers about health, law or money are shown for study. They are not medical, legal or financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is Sturch really free?

Yes. Sturch is free to use and needs no credit card. Donations are voluntary and go to the student who built it.

Where do the papers come from?

Sturch searches OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Crossref and Europe PMC at the same time. Sturch does not own any papers: all content belongs to the publishers and authors.

Can I read the full papers for free?

When a paper is open access, Sturch links to the free full text. Use the Open Access filter to show only those papers.

What is the Answer Finder?

Pick a question template such as “What causes”, “Effect of” or “Difference between”, type a topic, and Sturch shows sentences from real papers that answer it, with the paper each one came from. It only reads paper abstracts, so check the source before you rely on an answer.

Why did the Answer Finder show no answer?

Sturch only shows sentences that clearly answer your question, and it only reads abstracts. Very common words, very narrow topics or strict filters can return nothing. Try a more specific topic, a different question template, or fewer filters, or use Search Papers.

Is Sturch a Google Scholar alternative?

Yes, you can use it that way. Sturch is a separate, free website. It is not made by or connected to Google. Like Google Scholar, it helps you find scholarly papers, but it searches OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, Crossref and Europe PMC at once, and adds citations in four styles, Answer Finder, Source Tracer and Topic Finder.

What is new in version 1.0?

Sturch is no longer in beta. Version 1.0 is the first full release. It keeps improving, so results can still be incomplete and things may change. Donations and feedback help it improve.

What does the Source Tracer do?

Paste a quote or a claim and Sturch looks for the paper it most likely came from. If the source is paraphrased or not indexed, it may not be found, and it will say so.

Are the citations accurate?

References are generated automatically from paper metadata and can miss details such as volume, issue or page numbers. Always double-check them before you submit your work.

Will Sturch write my research paper?

No. Sturch helps you find, read and cite real papers. It does not write your research for you.
